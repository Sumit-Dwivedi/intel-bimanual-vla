## Discussion (not in the raw log above)

### A -- home-pose verdict: KEEP the current home keyframe, no change

The script's own `OVERALL HOME-POSE VERDICT: FAIL` line is mechanically
correct (it ANDs every gate together, including the approach-column check
below) but is not this report's final judgement -- read on.

**The three criteria the task brief names that MuJoCo's own real contact
solver can answer directly -- self-collision, arm-vs-table, cross-arm --
all PASS with a clean zero count.** This is the authoritative measure (real
contact detection, not a geometric proxy) and it is unambiguous: the current
`"home"` keyframe (`shoulder_pan=0, shoulder_lift=-1.2, elbow_flex=-1.6,
wrist_flex=0, wrist_roll=0`, ADR-026) produces **zero** self, cross-arm or
table contacts at `reset(seed=0)`. The only 4 contacts present are between
props and the table (plate's two geoms and their own natural resting
contact, etc.) -- expected and benign, not a defect.

**The pinch-point-above-table criterion also passes cleanly**, both arms,
with a large margin (+0.238 m) -- unsurprising at a folded rest pose where
the gripper sits well above the table, not at a grasp.

**The approach-column criterion is where this report makes a judgement
call, and states it plainly.** The task brief specifies "a vertical
cylinder above each prop out to +15 cm" but gives no radius for that
cylinder -- there is nothing to measure against without choosing one. Two
methodologies were tried (both kept in the script, see its module
docstring and `_prop_planar_radius_m`):

1. One constant, sized to the largest prop's own footprint (0.06 m, the
   plate's dish radius), applied to every prop uniformly.
2. Each prop's OWN measured planar reach (`_prop_planar_radius_m`, a
   bounding-sphere-based measurement of that specific prop's geometry).

Method (2) is more defensible (it does not overstate what a thin object
like a spoon needs) and is what the log above reports. Under it, **fork
(-0.0086 m) and spoon (-0.0327 m) show a small negative margin**; plate,
mug and water_bottle all clear comfortably (+0.02 to +0.12 m).

**Traced, not left as an opaque number.** In both cases the closest geometry
is the OTHER arm's own `*_shoulder` mesh (`armB_shoulder` for the fork/spoon
columns, since both props sit closer to arm B's side of the table).
Measured directly: at the home pose, `armB_shoulder`'s collision mesh sits
at world `(x=-0.00002, y=0.1843, z=0.4057)` with bounding-sphere radius
0.069 m -- i.e. the folded elbow/shoulder assembly swings up and inward
toward the table's y=0 centreline (not out at the arm's own y=0.25 base),
landing within the fork/spoon's 15 cm approach column in z and within about
10-14 cm of them in the horizontal plane. This is a genuine, measured
proximity, not a script bug -- but it is a **bounding-sphere proxy** (the
sum of two conservative circles), not a real mesh-vs-mesh check, and the
real contact solver above already confirms there is **no actual contact**
at this pose. Interpreted plainly: at the home pose, the OTHER arm's own
folded shoulder housing passes closer to the fork/spoon's overhead approach
path than to the plate/mug/bottle's, which is worth a future skill sequencer
being mindful of when both arms move concurrently near that corner of the
table -- it is not evidence the current home pose is unsafe at rest, since
the pose that produces it is exactly the one with zero real contacts.

**Verdict: the current home keyframe is KEPT, unmodified.** Three reasons,
not just one:
1. The three real-contact gates (the ones with an unambiguous, MuJoCo-
   verified answer) all pass cleanly.
2. ADR-026 already explored this pose space directly (rendering every sign
   combination of `shoulder_lift`/`elbow_flex` and counting real contacts)
   and found exactly one better-or-equal candidate, the current one; the
   only other symmetric candidate it tried (`+1.2/+1.6`) measured 13
   self-collisions per arm plus a table penetration -- strictly worse, not
   an escape from the approach-column near-miss either (a table-penetrating
   pose would fail every criterion in this report, not just one).
3. The approach-column near-miss is a self-defined heuristic with an
   unspecified radius, not one of the three MuJoCo-verified gates -- it is
   reported honestly (including the negative numbers) as a disclosed
   limitation for a later stage to account for, not treated as grounds to
   destabilize an otherwise-clean, previously-hard-won pose.

**The task brief's premise -- "start from Lab 8's all-zeros home" -- is
independently reconfirmed wrong on this machine, matching the brief's own
measurement almost exactly.** All-zeros: 23 total contacts, **19**
armA<->armB, deepest **-0.0597 m**. The cross-arm count (19) differs from
ADR-026's own original number (29) -- traced to ADR-028 (finger-pad
grasping fix, landed after ADR-026), which disabled several bulky jaw MESH
collision geoms at the wrist that were contributing some of ADR-026's
originally-counted contacts; the SAME deepest penetration (-0.0597 m,
`armA_wrist` vs `armB_wrist`, a non-jaw body pair ADR-028 never touched) is
unchanged, which is consistent with that explanation rather than a defect
in either measurement. The task brief's own count (19) matches this
session's fresh measurement exactly, so it is the current, correct number;
ADR-026's original 29/34 predate ADR-028 and are superseded by this
finding, not overwritten in their own entry (same "append a note, don't
edit history" convention ADR-026 itself used against ADR-025's numbers).

### B/C -- prop reachability and handoff corridor: CONFIRMED on bm-ptl,
one discrepancy in the water_bottle diagnostic reported

The hover-reachability table (fork/mug/plate OK at every height 0-10 cm,
both arms; water_bottle FAIL at every height, both arms) and the 5-height
handoff-corridor table (93/89/75/21/0 cells, matching x/y extents at every
row) **reproduce the task's own reported numbers exactly**, on bm-ptl, with
no discrepancy in any cell count or extent.

One number underneath the water_bottle verdict does NOT match: this
script's own diagnostic (`_diagnose_unreachable`, reusing
`ik_geometric.solve_topdown_ik`'s own internal shoulder-offset-circle
projection) measures the REQUIRED planar reach at 0.3046 m against a 2-link
annulus max of 0.2510 m (deficit ~5.4 cm), whereas the task's own figures
were 0.271 m required against ~0.249 m available (deficit ~2.2 cm). The
**verdict is identical either way** (unreachable, by both arms, at every
tested height, a genuine horizontal-reach problem) -- only the intermediate
numbers differ, most likely because this diagnostic uses the EXACT
shoulder-offset projection `solve_topdown_ik` itself solves against
(`r_perp = sqrt(dist_to_pan_axis**2 - d**2)`, which already subtracts the
~2 cm lateral offset described in ADR-070), while a simpler straight-line
distance from a nearby but not-identical reference point would read a few
centimetres lower. Flagged rather than silently reconciled, since the
source of the task's own two numbers is not available to this script to
check directly.

### C -- water_bottle decision: SCOPED OUT of v2, not moved

Two options were on the table (task brief): move `BOTTLE_POS` inward in
`gen_dual_scene.py` until both arms reach it with >= 3 cm margin, or declare
bottle skills out of scope for v2. **Decision: scope out, do not move the
prop, do not regenerate the scene.**

**Why not move it.** `gen_dual_scene.py`'s own history records a directly
relevant precedent: ADR-038 fix 3 moved four props' xy positions (including
the bottle's) for an unrelated (cosmetic, decluttering) reason, and a
bm-ptl regression run found it broke `handoff(A, B, fork)` at Phase 3, for
a reason a per-prop bisection could not isolate to any single moved prop --
only reverting ALL FOUR positions restored the previously-working skill.
That fix was reverted in full and `gen_dual_scene.py` carries an extended
comment on exactly this fragility. Moving `BOTTLE_POS` again, even by a few
centimetres and even for a well-motivated reason, carries the same
demonstrated risk of an unrelated regression elsewhere in the scene, and
this stage's own budget (2 hours) does not include time for the
full-skill regression sweep that would be needed to rule that out
responsibly.

**Why scoping out is acceptable here.** `pour` (the only skill that uses
`water_bottle`) is already the lowest-priority skill in this project's own
plan -- `PLAN.md`'s pre-committed cut ladder lists dropping `pour` as
rung 2, ahead of every other cut, and ADR-011/ADR-017 already scope it as a
tilt-pose-only skill with no fluid simulation, disclosed in the README. Master's
own baseline success rate for `pick(A, water_bottle)` (9/20, using `ik.py`'s
position-only solver, not the top-down solver this branch adds) is the
number this decision REGRESSES from restated plainly, not buried.

**The regression, stated once more, plainly:** under `ik_geometric`'s
top-down solver, `water_bottle` is unreachable by either arm at any tested
hover height (Section B/C above) -- a real capability loss versus master,
where the older, position-only `ik.py` solver could reach it (imperfectly,
9/20) with no top-down/orientation constraint at all. This is the direct
cost of adopting a stricter, exact top-down solver: it buys a closed-form,
sub-millimetre-accurate grasp for the objects it CAN reach (fork/mug/plate,
Section B), at the cost of a smaller reachable set overall (ADR-070 already
measured the top-down workspace at 35-53% of the position-only one) that
happens to exclude the one prop placed furthest from both arms' bases.
