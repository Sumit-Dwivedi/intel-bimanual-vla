## Caveat on the penetration filter — measured, not assumed

ADR-035's own warm-start diagnostic (Part 3) found a `table_top`-vs-arm-mesh contact reporting **-0.22211 m** penetration depth on a converged, physically sensible pose — a convex-hull mesh artifact, not real interpenetration. This script therefore records the raw worst-contact-depth per sample and reports rejection at two bars: the naive 1 mm bar (`NAIVE_TABLE_TOL_M=0.001`, matching `skills_scripted.TABLE_COLLISION_DEPTH_TOL_M`) and a loose 2 cm bar (`LOOSE_TABLE_TOL_M=0.02`).

## Interpretation: which cloud is authoritative

**RAW, not the 1mm- or 2cm-filtered cloud.** The sanity check is decisive, not merely
suggestive: `fork_at_rest` for arm A converges cleanly (residual 0.00907 m, essentially
identical to the 0.0091 m this exact skill target measures in the live regression gate,
`scripts/verify_adr038_skills.py`'s own `pick(A, fork)` check) and `pick(A, fork)` is a
real, working, regression-gated skill — yet the naive 1mm filter reports a **-0.39050 m**
`table_top` contact there and rejects it, and even the loose 2cm bar still rejects it. This
is a bigger version of exactly the artifact ADR-035 Part 3 found (-0.22211 m on a different
converged pose): the `type="mesh"` convex-hull collision check fires on physically-sensible,
demonstrably-working arm configurations near table height, at a magnitude far beyond
anything a real graze could produce. A filter that rejects a skill target the shipped
controller successfully uses every regression run cannot be trusted to define "reachable."

This is corroborated by *where* the raw and 1mm-filtered clouds actually disagree: at
z=0.35-0.43 m (closest to `TABLE_SURFACE_Z=0.35`, exactly where the mesh artifact fires),
the 1mm-filtered intersection is 3-4x smaller than the raw intersection (e.g. z=0.35:
269 vs. 805 densified intersection cells); from z=0.45 upward the two clouds are
byte-identical (the arm is no longer near the table, so the artifact never fires and there
is nothing left for the filter to reject). The filter's damage is concentrated exactly
where the known artifact lives and vanishes exactly where it shouldn't apply — that is
the filter behaving like a mesh-collision bug, not like a real reachability boundary.
**Every bound, grid and intersection number in this report's "explicit question" answer
below uses the RAW cloud.**

## A second confound, caught and corrected before answering: sampling density

The primary N=50000 run's own intersection table (the "raw cloud" subsection under
"Intersection" above) found a best rectangle of only **8 cm x 1 cm** (z=0.43) — small
enough, on its own, to look like "no shared region," which would have been the wrong
conclusion for the wrong reason. With only ~50000 samples split across 13 z-slices and a
footprint of order 1000-1300 occupied 1cm cells per arm per slice, the ASCII grids above
visibly show a scattered, salt-and-pepper occupancy pattern (see e.g. the z=0.35 grid): a
few samples per occupied cell on average is enough to fix min/max bounds reliably (bounds
are density-insensitive) but is NOT enough to trust a single-cell gap between two occupied
cells as a genuine hole rather than Monte Carlo noise — and the largest-rectangle
computation is exactly the kind of test a few stray gaps can wreck.

The densification check (N=300000 per arm, 6x, same method, same filter, fresh seeds)
resolves this: at every one of the 13 slices the intersection is a **large, stable, but
elongated band** (800-1100 of ~2600-2700 per-arm cells overlap at every single slice —
roughly 40% of each arm's own footprint — never a handful of scattered pixels), with a
largest axis-aligned rectangle that is consistently long-and-thin (2x46 at z=0.57, 24x3 at
z=0.47, 4x32 at z=0.51 — the overall best) rather than square, at every slice, both before
and after the 1mm filter (identical from z=0.45 up, per the point above). Going from 50000
to 300000 samples changed the absolute numbers a lot (8x1 cm to a real ~30-40 cm long band)
but did NOT change the qualitative shape: not one of the 13 slices, at either sample count,
ever produced a rectangle reaching 10 cm in *both* dimensions simultaneously. That
stability under a 6x density change is the evidence this is a real geometric property of
the two arms' overlapping reach envelopes (each individually annular/shell-shaped, since
neither arm can reach its own base — two overlapping annuli naturally intersect in a
crescent/band, not a disc), not a sampling artifact.

## The explicit question

**No.** At the current base separation (arm A at `y=-0.25`, arm B at `y=+0.25`,
`so101_dual_table.xml`), there is **no contiguous both-arms-reachable region of at least
10 cm x 10 cm at any tested height** (z = 0.35 to 0.59 m, every 2 cm, 13 slices) — checked
twice, at N=50000 and at a 6x-denser N=300000 cross-check, with the same answer both times
and the same answer under both the raw and the (less-trusted) 1mm-filtered cloud.

**The largest region that exists:** a band roughly **4 cm x 32 cm** (z=0.51 m, densified,
raw and 1mm-filtered identical there) — real, substantial in total area (~1046-1056
intersecting cells at that slice, about 40% of each arm's own reachable footprint at that
height), but only 4 cm across in its short axis, well under the 10 cm bar in that
dimension. Every other slice from 0.35 to 0.59 m shows the same shape: a long, thin band
(short axis 2-9 cm, long axis 8-46 cm), never a compact square.

**This does not contradict ADR-032/035/048's near-single-point findings — it explains
them, from one level up.** Those measurements additionally required simultaneous,
collision-free occupancy of both arms at once (both arms' solved configurations applied
together, checked for cross-arm contact) and found essentially a single point / zero-width
envelope. This measurement checks a strictly *weaker*, more permissive condition — each
arm's OWN kinematic reach, sampled independently, with the other arm held fixed at its home
pose and never checked against it at all — and even that weaker condition already fails to
contain a 10x10 cm square, at every height tested. Adding back the missing cross-arm
collision constraint (not measured here) can only shrink the true handoff-feasible region
further, never grow it. So the "single point" ADR-032/035 found and the "band too narrow
for 10x10 cm" this report finds are the same underlying fact seen at two different levels
of strictness, not two different facts in tension with each other — and per this task's own
calibration warning, that consistency is exactly what should be expected, not a large
intersection appearing "by mistake."

**What this means for Stage 2.** The shared region is not merely mis-placed within an
otherwise-adequate overlap (a per-prop or per-skill-target adjustment would not fix this):
its SHORT AXIS is fundamentally too narrow (never exceeding ~9 cm at any of the 13 heights
checked, purely from individual kinematic reach, before any collision constraint is even
applied) for a comfortable double-arm working zone anywhere in the sampled range. Moving
the drawer, a prop, or the single `HANDOFF_POSITION_XYZ` constant cannot widen a
fundamental shell-intersection geometry problem. Relocating one or both arm bases — the
change ADR-021's original ~0.30 m reach / 0.50 m base-gap layout assumption never
verified — is the change with the leverage to actually widen this band; this report does
not choose a new placement (out of this stage's scope, and doing so needs its own targeted
sweep now that the shape of the problem, not just its existence, is measured), but it does
close the "maybe the current separation is already fine and something else is the real
problem" question definitively: it is not fine, at any height, by a wide margin.
